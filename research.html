Research

I study how people and organizations collaborate to address public problems that cross political and functional boundaries.

Overview

My work asks how individuals and organizations collaborate to solve problems no single actor can solve alone. Public problems rarely respect organizational charts or jurisdictional lines, so the people and agencies working on them have to build relationships across those boundaries, and the structure of those relationships shapes what gets accomplished. I study how these networks form, what sustains them, and whether they actually improve outcomes, combining social network analysis with experimental, longitudinal, and causal inference methods.

This research has been supported by the National Science Foundation, the National Institutes of Health, the Department of Health and Human Services, the Federal Transit Administration, and USAID.

Advice, learning, and performance inside organizations

Who do public employees actually turn to for advice, and does it matter? This strand examines how informal networks inside schools and public agencies shape learning, performance, and commitment — often diverging sharply from the formal structures meant to govern them.

Interlocal collaboration and regional governance

Local governments face complex problems — transportation, flooding, climate adaptation, service delivery — that spill across their borders. This work examines when they choose to collaborate, who they partner with, how those arrangements are structured and contracted, and whether the resulting networks perform.

Network theory, methods, and causal inference

Network data is expensive to collect and network claims are notoriously difficult to make causally. This strand develops approaches to measuring structure and asks how network science can support credible inference and pragmatic intervention.

Crisis, disaster, and emergency response

Extreme events force organizations that have never worked together to coordinate under severe time pressure. This research traces how response networks form in the moment, how information moves through them, and what distinguishes systems that adapt from those that fail.

Networks, health, and community wellbeing

Recent collaborative work applies network methods to population health and environmental equity: how safety practices spread through farmworker crews, how social ties shape adolescent wellbeing, and how environmental burden concentrates in particular communities.
